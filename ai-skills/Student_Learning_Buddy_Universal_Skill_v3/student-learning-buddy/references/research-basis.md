# Evidence informing the teaching policy
Research date: 25 September 2026. Separate observed source content, learning evidence and design hypotheses. Indexed YouTube reach is a selection proxy, not a measure of learning. This review used accessible video pages, descriptions, chapter lists and an official lesson adaptation; it was not a complete audiovisual or viewer-comment audit. Do not claim the selected videos are a globally verified best-performing ranking or that viewers' motives were directly measured.

They need testing with the intended learners. No IQ grouping, universal six-minute attention limit, perfect multilingual coverage or guaranteed educational effectiveness is supported.

## Second-pass research and audit, 25 September 2026
The second pass added two discovery candidates and tightened the operational implications. It did not remove the earlier evidence limits.

- [Math Antics — Fractions Are Parts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CA9XLJpQp3c): the indexed YouTube page showed approximately 2.6M views; the [YouTube Music rendering](https://music.youtube.com/watch?v=CA9XLJpQp3c) showed a 5:16 duration. The [official catalogue](https://www.mathantics.com/) separates fraction topics and offers videos/worksheets. Full audiovisual content was not accessible in this review. Transferable design hypothesis: make a single concept navigable and pair explanation with optional application. Do not claim its exact humour, pacing or visual technique was inspected.
- [Dear Sir — creator milestone video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KbyPJ_qvagk): the creator reported 30 million views for a tense lesson and linked [that lesson](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pXZtRXpGNck). This is a historical creator-reported milestone, not a current independently audited count or proof of the creator's “most watched” claim. The [grammar revision video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=25lG5C7ygW4) provides sections for rules and practice questions. Design hypothesis: bridge understandable explanation to an exam/practice format, without copying promotional promises. Full delivery and learner outcomes were not verified.

Do not attribute student preferences to these videos without evidence. A future field study should ask learners to compare matched-topic explanations and test independent application and delayed recall. Viewer comments alone would be self-selected evidence. The audit found that explicit answer formatting, language precedence, mixed-register output, false-premise correction and conversation continuity were under-specified in the first skill; these were engineering gaps, not findings about the videos.

## Teacher-analysis rubric for future source reviews
Record these dimensions separately rather than giving one unsupported “best teacher” score:
1. Discovery: topic, audience, language, upload date, observed reach and collection date.
2. Hook: whether the first segment names the doubt, motivates it, or merely promotes the channel.
3. Prerequisites: assumed vocabulary, notation and missing foundational steps.
4. Mechanism: causal or mathematical reasoning versus memorized procedure.
5. Examples: familiarity, representational mapping, exception cases and stereotype risk.
6. Pacing: conceptual chunk boundaries and where learners can pause; avoid inferring a universal ideal length.
7. Visuals: whether they explain a relationship or decorate it; include accessibility needs.
8. Language/register: explanation language, technical vocabulary, code-switching and respectful familiarity.
9. Participation: predictions, worked examples, independent attempts and feedback opportunities.
10. Error handling: misconceptions named and repaired versus simply showing a correct answer.
11. Motivation: learner agency and relevant success versus fear, exaggerated promises or dependency.
12. Learning evidence: independent application, transfer and delayed recall; distinguish from views, likes and testimonials.

For each dimension, label “observed,” “inferred,” or “not accessible.” No claim of full video analysis should be made when only title, description or chapter metadata was accessible. Keep the learner-facing skill original; use general teaching methods rather than reproducing a creator's scripts or identity.
